\title{\bf dSIBP: a package for generating IBP seeds for QFT in de Sitter spacetime}

\author{a}
\affiliation{a}

\abstract{
\texttt{dSIBP} is a \textsc{Mathematica} package that generates the integration-by-parts (IBP)
relations of Feynman integrals in de Sitter (dS) spacetime. The input is a Feynman graph whose
internal lines are massive or massless and live on the two Schwinger--Keldysh branches. The package
builds the integral family of this graph, writes its canonical IBP seeds, and assembles them into a
sealed linear system for the external reducers Kira and Rational Tracer. One further command
differentiates the reduced masters and returns their differential equations, together with Euler
scaling identities that check the result. A parallel track treats tree-level families, which carry no
loop momentum. The package presents no new derivation; all formulas come from the existing de Sitter
IBP constructions. Its usage is described through worked examples, from the sunrise graph to a mixed
loop-plus-tree family with eighty-one master integrals.
}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Correlation functions of quantum fields in de Sitter (dS) spacetime are the basic observables of
inflationary perturbation theory. They are computed in the in-in formalism, whose diagrammatic form
is the Schwinger--Keldysh path integral \cite{Chen:2017ryl}. After the Feynman rules are applied, a
graph becomes scalar integrals over the conformal times $\tau_v<0$ of its vertices. The integrand is
a product of vertex phases $e^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma_v E_v\tau_v}$, where $\sigma_v=\pm$ is the
Schwinger--Keldysh branch sign of vertex $v$ and $E_v$ is its energy, and of propagator functions.
Massive internal
states make these integrals hard, because their propagators are written with Hankel functions, so
the integrand is no longer of polynomial type. Results with massive states are still sparse.
Ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp} states the situation directly: explicit computations of such correlators
remain largely limited to tree level and to bubble topologies at one loop.

The route taken in this paper comes from flat-spacetime QFT, where the same problem has a standard
solution. Integration by parts (IBP) \cite{Chetyrkin:1981qh} expresses every integral of a family as
a linear combination of a small set of master integrals, and Laporta's algorithm made this reduction
automatic \cite{Laporta:2000dsw}. Differentiating the masters and reducing again gives a closed
system of first-order linear differential equations (DE)
\cite{Kotikov:1990kg,Kotikov:1991pm}. That method produced the dimensionally regulated pentagon
integrals \cite{Bern:1993kr} and the two-loop four-point functions \cite{Gehrmann:1999as}, and it
becomes much simpler when the DE are written in $d\log$ form \cite{Henn:2013pwa}.

What made this route decisive is not only the algebra but also the software built around it. Seed
generation and reduction are automated by FIRE \cite{Smirnov:2019qkx,Smirnov:2025prc} and by Kira
\cite{Maierhofer:2017gsa,Klappert:2020nbg}, and rational-function reconstruction by Rational Tracer
\cite{Magerya:2022hvj}. The DE are solved numerically by DiffExp \cite{Hidding:2020ytt} and by the
auxiliary mass flow method with its implementation AMFlow \cite{Liu:2017jxz,Liu:2022chg}. This last
series also shows how little input the whole scheme needs. Reference \cite{Liu:2022mfb} finds that
Feynman integrals with any number of loops are completely determined once linear relations among
them are given, and it verifies this on examples up to five loops. Computing an integral then becomes
linear algebra plus numerical transport, with no integration input left to supply. In practice the
user writes down a topology, and a program handles the rest.

The same two steps were set up for dS space in
refs.~\cite{Chen:2023iix,Chen:2024glu,Chen:2026dqp}. Reference \cite{Chen:2023iix} extends IBP from
polynomial integrands to the Hankel integrands of dS. It proves universal formulas for the iterative
reduction and for the $d\log$-form DE of an arbitrary vertex integral family, and it states that
these formulas dominate all tree-level dS correlators and play a kernel role at loop level.
Reference \cite{Chen:2024glu} solves such equations by power series, gives multivariate
hypergeometric expressions for the homogeneous part of an arbitrary tree, and shows how to determine
the boundary data. Reference \cite{Chen:2026dqp} treats the loop-momentum integrals as well. It
identifies a structural property of the dS IBP system: an $n$-propagator family splits into $2^n$
closed subsystems, classified by the parity of the propagator indices. Its explicit construction is
carried out for the one-loop bubble family.

These works also point at the same missing layer. Reference \cite{Chen:2023iix} observes that
existing flat-space programs can be reused, with Kira solving the IBP reduction of a user-defined
system, and it leaves the boundary data of the DE outside its scope. Reference \cite{Chen:2024glu}
notes that the automatic tools widely used in flat QFT could help. None of the three releases a
program that turns a general dS graph into that user-defined system. Assembling the system by hand
is laborious and error-prone already at one loop. A massive line carries three integer labels: a
propagator power and two Hankel powers. Massive lines obey second-order equations of motion, so only
two of their vertex states are independent. Massless full lines must be treated as a quotient
over vertex states. The parity classification just recalled splits the system into independent blocks,
and one block is enough per reduction.

\texttt{dSIBP} is a \textsc{Mathematica} package that automates this step. The user declares a
Feynman graph by its vertices, by the four Schwinger--Keldysh types of its internal propagators,
and by its external momenta. The package then performs the following tasks.

\begin{itemize}
\item It validates the kinematic declarations, namely the loop momenta, the no-loop external
magnitudes and the rules that bind scalar products to coordinates. It reports whether the user lists
are complete or over-complete.
\item It constructs the integral family $J$ with structured labels for propagator powers, vertex
states and user-defined irreducible scalar products (ISPs).
\item It generates canonical IBP seed equations, from the time generators of each sector and from
the $L(L+K)$ loop-momentum generators, where $L$ is the loop number of the graph and $K$ the number
of loop-related external momenta. The massive vertex states are restricted to the
proven representative set $\{0,1\}$.
\item It sweeps the continuous labels over a user-specified envelope and assembles a sealed,
backend-neutral linear system.
\item It exports the system to Kira \cite{Maierhofer:2017gsa,Klappert:2020nbg} or Rational Tracer
\cite{Magerya:2022hvj} for the actual reduction, and it imports the results back.
\item It differentiates the reduced master integrals with respect to the independent kinematic
variables. This yields differential equations, optionally in $d\log$ form. Euler scaling identities
provide an independent check of the result.
\end{itemize}

Two boundaries of the package are worth stating. It generates and serializes IBP relations, but it
never runs a reduction itself. The reduction is delegated to external programs, which keeps the
package independent of any particular reduction engine. The package also adds no new derivation:
every formula it uses is derived in refs.~\cite{Chen:2023iix,Chen:2024glu,Chen:2026dqp}. What it
supplies instead is the notation those formulas need in order to be applied to an arbitrary graph,
and a machine-readable form of the system they generate. Every symbol that appears in the output of
the package is traced back to its definition.

A complementary track handles the time-only families of graphs without cycles, which carry no loop
momentum. Its seeds can be reduced internally to differential equations and to $d\log$ forms. This
track interfaces with
the package MadStree \cite{MadStree:inpreparation}, which evaluates tree-level cosmological
correlators numerically. Related differential structures for cosmological loop integrands appear in
the kinematic-flow construction \cite{Baumann:2024mvm,Hang:2024xas}, which is not
implemented here.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:notation} fixes the notation: the
Schwinger--Keldysh Feynman rules, the integral family and its labels, the kinematic coordinates, the
parity structure, the massive function systems and the time-only representation. Section~\ref{sec:using}
describes the public interface, from initialization to seeds, linear systems, external reduction and
differential equations, and then the tree-level track. Section~\ref{sec:examples} walks through the
examples shipped with the package, including a mixed loop-plus-tree family whose reduction system
holds more than eight hundred thousand relations. Section~\ref{sec:summary} closes with an outlook.
Appendix~\ref{app:api} lists the public functions with one-line descriptions.

\begin{itemize}

the same reference.
This task validates the IBP relations and the differential equations.
\item \emph{Massive three-vertex tree family.} The formulas were first derived independently by an
agent, which generalized an earlier hand-written two-vertex implementation. They were checked

validates the tree-level IBP relations and the tree-level differential equations.
\item \emph{One-loop bubble, full pipeline.} The IBP system is reduced by an external Kira run,
which closes with no unreduced target and returns nineteen masters. The two $19\times19$
differential-equation matrices keep both derivative variables symbolic. At the recorded exact point
they reproduce the matrices of ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp} entry by entry, $361/361$ for each of the
three variable conventions. The scaling identities hold on the reduced basis: $53/53$ source-level
identities and $19/19$ active-basis checks pass symbolically. This task validates the IBP relations,
the differential equations and the scaling identities.
\item \emph{Mixed bubble-plus-tree family, full pipeline.} The family has eighty-one master
integrals. The external Kira reduction closes with no unreduced target. The six $81\times81$
differential-equation matrices are free of residual symbols. All $81/81$ source-level Euler scaling
and IBP identities vanish exactly. These identities are independent of the numerical reduction. The
reduction itself carries a scaling certificate at a recorded exact point. This task
validates the IBP relations, the differential equations and the scaling identities.
\item \emph{General formulas against naive sampling.} The package substitutes the derived general
formulas instead of solving IBP systems again. Its output was compared with reductions of naively
sampled raw IBP seeds of the same families. The IBP relations and the differential equations agree.
This task validates the IBP relations and the differential equations.
\item \emph{Tree-level differential equations against MadStree.} The iterative route and the naive

All seventy-five derivatives closed, and the five $15\times15$ differential-equation matrices agree
entry by entry with the direct $d\log$ formula route of MadStree. This task validates the

\end{itemize}

\section{Notation and conventions}
\label{sec:notation}

\subsection{Schwinger--Keldysh Feynman rules}
\label{sec:skrules}

A de Sitter Feynman graph in the in-in formalism consists of vertices $v$ with conformal time
$\tau_v<0$ and Schwinger--Keldysh branch signs $\sigma_v=\pm$. Each vertex with external energy
$E_v$ carries the phase factor
\begin{equation}
e^{-\mathrm{i} \sigma_v E_v \tau_v}\,,
\label{eq:vertexPhase}
\end{equation}
and the time integrations run over the full interval $\tau_v\in(-\infty,0)$. An internal propagator
$e$ joining the ordered vertices $u$ and $v$ is one of four types. It is massive or massless, and it
is \emph{full} when the two branch signs agree, $\sigma_u=\sigma_v$, and \emph{cross} when they
differ, $\sigma_u=-\sigma_v$. Only a full propagator contains a Heaviside ordering of the two vertex
times, so only a full propagator produces theta-shrink terms. The signs of the two endpoints do not
give further types: $++$ and $--$ are the same full propagator, and $+-$ and $-+$ are the same cross
propagator. A massless full propagator does not distinguish its two endpoints, and section
\ref{sec:family} writes out the quotient it satisfies. In our figures a circle denotes a vertex, and
its fill colour encodes the branch sign: black is $\sigma_v=+$, white is $\sigma_v=-$, and gray
leaves the sign general. The conformal time $\tau_v$ is written below the
vertex and the vertex energy beside it. External legs end in small open squares. Each vertex carries
one energy $E_v$, the energy that appears in its phase factor. It is a symbol of the declaration: the
user may bind it to the packaged magnitudes of the external legs attached to $v$, or keep it
independent even when no external leg is attached to it. Figure~\ref{fig:skrules} shows these
rules for the bubble input of the
package. Its two vertices are drawn in gray because their branch signs are general. Vertex~1
carries the external momenta $k_{1,1},\dots,k_{1,n_1}$ and vertex~2 carries
$k_{2,1},\dots,k_{2,n_2}$. The internal momenta are $q$ and $q-k$.

The package distinguishes the four propagator types as
$\langle$\texttt{massive, massless}$\rangle\times\langle$\texttt{full, cross}$\rangle$.
Figure~\ref{fig:linetypes} shows the resulting graphical conventions. Every propagator that lies
on a cycle becomes a denominator of the integral family. A propagator that does not lie on a cycle
is a \emph{fixed propagator}, and the package also calls such a line a \emph{bridge}. It carries
only a magnitude power and no loop-momentum denominator. Massive propagators whose two vertices
become coincident under a contact operation
may be \emph{shrunk}. Shrunk and fixed propagators survive only through the structured
normalisation $N_s$ of the sector, defined in section~\ref{sec:family}.

\subsection{The integral family}
\label{sec:family}

For a sector $s$, that is, a subset of propagators retained as denominators, a generic member of
the integral family is a time-ordered integral over the vertex times,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
J[s,\{a_v\},\{b_e,n_{e,1},n_{e,2}\},\{\rho_r\}]
={}&N_s\!\int_{-\infty}^{0}\prod_v d\tau_v\;
\prod_v (-\tau_v)^{A_v}\, e^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma_v E_v \tau_v}\\
&\times\prod_{e\in s} \mathcal{G}_e(\tau_u,\tau_v)^{B_e}\;
\prod_{\substack{e\in s\\ \mathrm{massive}}} H_e^{n_{e,1}}\,\bar H_e^{n_{e,2}}\;
\prod_r \rho_r^{\,i_r}\,.
\end{split}
\label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
Here $e=(u,v)$ runs over the propagators retained by the sector and $\mathcal{G}_e$ is the
corresponding Schwinger--Keldysh propagator function. The factors $\rho_r$ are the irreducible scalar
products (ISPs) that the user declares, defined in section~\ref{sec:kinematics}, and $i_r$ are their
integer powers. The powers that enter the integrand are the
\emph{actual} powers $A_v=a_v+a0_v$ and $B_e=b_e+b0_e$, built from the package-level labels $a_v$
and $b_e$ and from the zero points $a0_v$ and $b0_e$ described below. $H_e$ and $\bar H_e$ are the
two Hankel-function blocks of a massive propagator, one at each of its vertices, and a massless
propagator has no such block. The factor $N_s$ is the structured normalisation of the sector. It
collects the magnitude powers that no longer sit in a denominator, namely the power of every fixed
propagator and the power kept by every shrunk propagator. It is a product of powers of the
independent no-loop magnitudes of section~\ref{sec:kinematics}, multiplied by a constant part built
from the Wronskian normalisations of the shrunk massive lines. Derivatives act on $N_s$ like on any
other factor of the integrand.
In the package the family is written in the three-slot schema
\begin{verbatim}
J[aList, linePacks, ispList]
\end{verbatim}
The first slot is the list of vertex time powers $a_v$ of the vertices that are still distinct in
this sector, the \emph{active} vertices of the sector, in the frozen vertex order. The second slot
holds one pack per propagator, in the order
in which the declaration key \texttt{"lines"} lists them, and the shapes of the packs identify the
sector. The third slot lists the ISPs with their integer powers $i_r$. A case is one named
declaration. Its keys give the vertices with their branch types, the propagators with their
endpoints, momenta and mass types, the external legs, the loop momenta, and the two lists of
external momenta. They also carry the zero points, the symmetry rules and the parity constraints
discussed below.

\begin{itemize}
\item A cyclic massive full, massive cross or massless full propagator carries
$\{b_e,n_{e,1},n_{e,2}\}$: the power $b_e$ and the two state powers.
\item A cyclic massless cross propagator contributes $\{b_e,0,0\}$. It is a single exponential
product, so it has no discrete state.
\item A fixed propagator contributes $\{\texttt{"F"},n_{e,1},n_{e,2}\}$: no denominator power, and
its magnitude power sits in $N_s$.
\item A shrunk propagator contributes $\{b_{S,e}\}$, or $\{\texttt{"F"}\}$ when it was already
fixed. The power $b_{S,e}$ is kept by $N_s$.
\end{itemize}

The zero points belong to the declaration. A case supplies them through the key
\texttt{zeroPointRules}, as rules for $a0_v$ and $b0_e$. Two settings of this key are used by the
released examples. The sunrise and the mixed case keep them as free regulator symbols, for example
$a0_v=\alpha_v$ and $b0_e=\beta_e$. The pure massive bubble fixes them instead: for a line of mass
index $\nu$ under the \texttt{"h"} preset of section~\ref{sec:dynamics} it takes $b0_e=-2\nu$, and at
the vertices it takes $a0_v=2\nu+\texttt{analyticRegulator}$. The last symbol is kept during seed
generation and set to zero afterwards. It is what the initialization audit of
section~\ref{sec:init} asks for when the small-time leading powers of a family reach a divergent
time-integral boundary under integer index shifts. With
the zero points fixed this way, the labels $a_v,b_e,n_{e,i}$ that the package manipulates run over
small integers. They make the IBP shifts uniform: the generators shift the package-level labels by
$\pm1$ and never touch the zero points.

\paragraph{Massive vertex states and equations of motion.}
Every massive propagator is assigned a second-order function system
$f''+P(x)f'+Q(x)f=0$. Its variable is $x=-\xi\tau$, the negative of the product of the momentum
magnitude $\xi$ of the line and the vertex time $\tau$, and primes are derivatives with respect to
$x$. Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} lists the presets. A massive propagator $e$ with mass index $\nu_e$
and the \texttt{"h"} preset $P_{\mathrm h}(x)=(2\nu_e+1)/x$,
$Q_{\mathrm h}(x)=1$ has Hankel blocks at each of its two vertices that span a two-dimensional space.
The space is
indexed by the state $n_{e,i}$, where $i=1,2$ names the endpoint of $e$, and $a_{e,i}$ denotes the
time power of that same endpoint. The second-order equation of motion gives, at $n_{e,i}=2$, the
exact relation
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
J\big[\cdots,n_{e,i}=2,\cdots\big]
={}&-J\big[\cdots,n_{e,i}=0,\cdots\big]\\
&-(2\nu_e+1)\,J\big[\cdots,n_{e,i}=1,\,a_{e,i}\!\to\!a_{e,i}\!-\!1,\,b_e\!\to\!b_e\!+\!1,\cdots\big],
\end{split}
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
so every massive vertex state reduces to the representative set $\{0,1\}$. The package applies
this reduction by construction: the generated seeds contain only $n_{e,i}\in\{0,1\}$.

\paragraph{Massless quotient.}
A massless full propagator $e$ between the ordered vertices $u$ and $v$ does not distinguish its two
endpoints. Let $\Delta=\tau_u-\tau_v$ and let $q$ be the magnitude of its momentum. Its kernel is
built from the two blocks
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
M_0={}&\theta(\Delta)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta}
      +\theta(-\Delta)\,e^{+\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta},\\
M_1={}&-\theta(\Delta)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta}
      +\theta(-\Delta)\,e^{+\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta},
\end{split}
\label{eq:masslessBlocks}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the common branch sign of the two endpoints, so $\sigma=+1$ on $++$ and
$\sigma=-1$ on $--$. Exchanging the two vertices leaves $M_0$ unchanged and flips the sign of $M_1$.
Write $F_{n_1n_2}$ for the family member with
$(n_{e,1},n_{e,2})=(n_1,n_2)$. The package works with the quotient
\begin{equation}
F_{00}=M_0,\qquad F_{10}=M_1,\qquad
F_{01}=-F_{10},\qquad F_{11}=-F_{00},
\label{eq:masslessQuotient}
\end{equation}
so only the two representatives $F_{00}$ and $F_{10}$ are independent. The pack of such a line still
keeps both labels, and each of them takes the values $0$ and $1$. Seeds are generated for the two
representatives only, and a state produced by a derivative is reduced back with $n_{e,2}\to0$. This is
an exact algebraic identification, not a sampling prescription.

\paragraph{Shrink rules.}
A massive propagator may be shrunk only in the \texttt{massiveFull} type and only when
$n_{e,1}+n_{e,2}=1$. Then exactly one endpoint state is non-zero, and if $i$ is that endpoint the
shrink multiplies the sector by the coefficient
$-\chi_e(-1)^{n_{e,i}}C_e$, where $\chi_e=\pm1$ is fixed by the two vertex types and
$C_e=\tfrac{4\mathrm{i}}{\pi}e^{\pi\,\mathrm{Im}\,\nu_e}$ is the Wronskian prefactor of the line. A
massless full propagator shrinks with a $\mp2$ delta term and a $+2$ term, without a Wronskian
prefactor and without label shifts. Several lines may shrink at once when their theta supports
coincide at one vertex. Let $B_v$ be this set of lines at the vertex $v$, and let $D_e$ denote the
shrink coefficient of the line $e$ just given. The contact terms are then
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\substack{\emptyset\neq Q\subseteq B_v\\ |Q|\ \mathrm{odd}}} 2^{\,1-|Q|}
\Big(\prod_{e\in Q} D_e\Big)\,
J\big[\cdots,\ e\in Q\ \text{shrunk},\cdots\big],
\label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
so the sum runs over the non-empty odd subsets of $B_v$, and each subset contributes one integral
whose lines are shrunk. Shrunk propagators never reappear as denominators. Their magnitude powers are
collected in $N_s$.

\subsection{Kinematics}
\label{sec:kinematics}

The structural loop number $L$ is the first Betti number of the internal undirected multigraph.
Let $N_E$ count the internal propagators, each one counted once: parallel propagators between the
same pair of vertices therefore contribute separately, and a self-loop contributes one.
External legs do not contribute to $N_E$. Let $N_V$ count the vertices and $C$
the connected components. Then
\begin{equation}
L=N_E-N_V+C\,.
\label{eq:loopnumber}
\end{equation}
Together with $K$ independent
loop-related external momenta, the loop number fixes the number of scalar products,
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{sp}}=\frac{L(L+1)}{2}+LK\,.
\label{eq:nsp}
\end{equation}
The user
declares two separate lists. The first list, \texttt{loopExternalMomenta} $\{k_1,\dots,k_K\}$,
defines the loop coordinates $\texttt{ss}ij=\sqrt{\mathrm{sp}[k_i,k_j]}$ of the loop Gram matrix.
The second list, \texttt{independentExternalMomenta}, defines the no-loop magnitudes
$|\texttt{kE}i|$ that appear in vertex energies and in the masses of fixed propagators. These
magnitudes are
parameterised by $\texttt{sE}i$. Only
magnitudes are coordinates. The package never outputs $\mathrm{sp}[k_{Ei},k_{Ej}]$, and it never
eliminates intermediate magnitudes by a cosine rule. Magnitudes that depend on the declared ones
are stored as explicit bindings. They still contribute to vertex phases through the chain rule.

User-defined ISPs are declared as \texttt{name/expr/range} associations, for example
\begin{verbatim}
<|"name" -> rho1, "expr" -> sp[q1, k], "range" -> {0, 1}|>,
\end{verbatim}
and they must form an invertible coordinate system on the space of scalar products together with
the propagator squares. The definition zero point of an ISP is fixed at $0$: positive powers are
numerator powers, and a negative power means an extra denominator.

The kinematic coordinates of a case are fixed by rules that express the basic scalar products in
terms of chosen symbols. A rule has the form
\texttt{sp[k1,k1] -> u\textasciicircum2}, and a rule can bind a whole energy as well, as
\texttt{sp[k1,k1] -> (E0-sE2)\textasciicircum2} does in the mixed example of
section~\ref{sec:examples}. The set of these rules is the kinematic system of the case, and its
symbols are exactly the variables that later derivatives and scaling checks use. The two-stage
interface \texttt{DSKinematics}/\texttt{DSInit}
validates this system and reports an exact, over-complete or under-complete
state. Only the exact state opens derivative generation.

\subsection{Parity structure}
\label{sec:parity}

The characteristic discrete invariant of de Sitter IBP is the parity of the sum of the labels on a
propagator. For every propagator $e$ that carries a denominator power, that is for every line of the
cycle skeleton, the quantity
\begin{equation}
p_e \;=\; b_e+n_{e,1}+n_{e,2} \pmod 2
\label{eq:parity}
\end{equation}
is conserved by every time generator and every loop-momentum generator. A massless cross line has
$n_{e,1}=n_{e,2}=0$, so its label is just $b_e$. A fixed propagator carries no denominator power, so it
enters no parity label, and the package rejects a constraint that names one. The default \texttt{"h"}
and \texttt{"H"} shrinks of a massive line, defined in section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, leave the parity
unchanged: such a shrink raises the power to $b_e+1$ and removes the single state power it consumes. A
massless contact keeps the power at $b_e$, so it offsets the parity of the child sector by one, and
several contacts accumulate modulo two. The package therefore does not apply a constraint to a sector
below the top sector in its original form: it rewrites the constraint in the labels of that sector,
using the power and zero-point shifts of its shrunk lines, and it disables the filter when such a
rewrite is not integral. The parity vector $(p_e)_e$ of the top sector therefore labels independent
blocks of the IBP system of a family, and for a graph whose
propagators all lie on the cycle skeleton this is exactly the split stated by
ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp}. For families with
user-provided \texttt{parityConstraints} the package keeps only the seeds whose transported constraint
vanishes. A constraint is written in the labels of \eqref{eq:parity}, for example
$b_1+n_{1,1}+n_{1,2}\equiv0$ for the propagator with declaration index $1$; the declaration spells
the same labels as \texttt{b[1]}, \texttt{n[1,1]} and \texttt{n[1,2]}. ISP indices receive no
parity filter.

\subsection{Massive function systems}
\label{sec:dynamics}

Every massive propagator is assigned a second-order function system
$f''+P(x)\,f'+Q(x)\,f=0$ in the variable $x=-\xi\tau$ of section~\ref{sec:family}. The default
\texttt{"h"} preset uses $P_{\mathrm h}=(2\nu_e+1)/x$ and
$Q_{\mathrm h}=1$. The package also accepts bare Hankel functions, the preset \texttt{"H"}, and
general user systems $(P,Q,T,W)$.
Let $T_e$ be the transport matrix of the fundamental system and $W_e$ its Wronskian. The package
tracks the combination $W_T=\det(T)\,W$ through all shrinks. The theta-coincidence and Wronskian
shrink terms used by the time-IBP generators are derived from it. Ordinary derivatives of the
kinematic coordinates never generate theta shrinks and never invoke $W_T$. The user chooses the
convention
once at initialization. The IBP generators, the derivative operators and the tree-level track all
read the same compiled function system.

\subsection{Tree-level and time-only families}
\label{sec:timeonly}

For graphs without cycles the package exposes a \texttt{timeOnly} family with the unique public
representation
\begin{verbatim}
J[sectorKey_String, timeShifts_List, stateBits_List]
\end{verbatim}
Here the \emph{root topology} is the declared graph before any contact, and its propagator order is
frozen for the whole family. The \texttt{sectorKey} records, for each propagator of the root
topology, whether it is full (1)
or shrunk (0). The list \texttt{timeShifts} gives the time powers $a_v$ of the active vertices. The
list \texttt{stateBits} gives one bit for each massive vertex state or massless quotient state, in
the frozen order of the root propagators. There are no momentum generators, no propagator powers
and no ISPs. The magnitude powers of fixed propagators remain in $N_s$
and still enter derivative generation. This track is for acyclic graphs only. A non-cyclic
propagator inside a graph that does have a cycle stays on the loop track, where it is a fixed
propagator. The massive-only time-only families are fully certified. A
certification gate controls the remaining cases, and the public interface exposes no way around it.
Families that contain massless full propagators are returned as \texttt{PendingRederivation}, and
their recorded reason is \texttt{masslessQuotientFormulaNotCertified}.

\section{Using the package}
\label{sec:using}

\subsection{Initialization}
\label{sec:init}

A case is the named declaration described in section~\ref{sec:family}. Initialization runs a
structural audit of the loop number,
the registry of vertices and propagators and the external-momentum routing. It then runs the
kinematic audit of
section~\ref{sec:kinematics}. It also certifies the time-integration domain: if an integer shift of
the continuous labels drives the leading small-time powers of a vertex to a divergent boundary, the
run stops and names the vertices that need a symbolic regulator. The result is a context that every
later command consumes:
\begin{verbatim}
context = DSInit[case, KinematicRules -> custom];
\end{verbatim}
The optional two-stage interface \texttt{DSKinematics[case]} first returns a proposal with the
default rules and a selection template. The user can then inspect the kinematic coordinates
$\texttt{ss}ij,\texttt{sE}i$, the momentum-routing audit and the capability flags before committing.
Under-complete declarations abort the initialization. Over-complete ones allow symbolic seed
generation but disable derivatives and the inner-form rewriting. In the over-complete case common
loop shifts are quotiented out through an affine basis \texttt{effectiveLoopExternalMomenta}.

\subsection{Seeds and the IBP envelope}
\label{sec:seeds}

The sectors of a family are ordered by how much of the declared graph they keep. The \emph{top
sector} keeps every propagator as a denominator, and the sectors below it arise from contacts that
shrink propagators and therefore make vertices coincide. The labels of the top sector are the root
labels used below.
\texttt{DSSeeds[context]} returns the canonical seed templates of the family. Every sector
receives all the time generators of its active vertices and all $L(L+K)$ loop-momentum generators.
The massive vertex
states are pre-reduced by the equation of motion \eqref{eq:eom}. Massless full propagators run over
the two quotient representatives of \eqref{eq:masslessQuotient}. The discrete $n$ labels are
eliminated, and the analytic seeds keep
general continuous labels $a_v,b_e,b_{S,e}$ and general ISP indices. Each template is hash-sealed.
Templates that the equation of motion and the canonicalization reduce to the identity $0=0$ are
retained and audited rather than dropped. The canonicalization applies the symmetry rules of the
declaration, where the user names a vertex swap or an exchange of parallel propagators, together with
built-in tadpole rules. A tadpole rule is available only for a self-loop whose reversal is a single
loop-momentum inversion; it either exchanges the two endpoint states of that line or maps the
integral to zero when an ISP carries an odd power.

The continuous labels are swept by \texttt{DSGenerateIBP}. The unified form
\begin{verbatim}
ibpData = DSGenerateIBP[allSeeds, {-1, 1}];
\end{verbatim}
requires that every root continuous index lies in the closed integer interval $[-1,1]$ \emph{after}
all shifts of the generated relations. The package inverts the per-index shift sets and derives the
narrower seed ranges automatically. The per-index form
\begin{verbatim}
ibpData = DSGenerateIBP[allSeeds,
   {a[v1], -1, 1}, {a[v2], 0, 1},
   {b[e1], 0, 2}, {b[e2], -1, 1}];
\end{verbatim}
must cover all root continuous indices exactly. ISP lower bounds are combined with the user target
by taking the larger lower bound, so the package never extends a range on its own in order to cover
numerator terms. The output is a sealed contract with status \texttt{"generated"}, a template-set
hash, an integrity audit and the capability flags \texttt{sealedQ}, \texttt{completeSystemQ} and
\texttt{subsetQ}. Only sealed complete systems may proceed to a formal reduction.

\subsection{Linear systems and external reduction}
\label{sec:linear}

\texttt{DSLinear[ibpData, context]} converts the sealed seeds into a backend-neutral association
\texttt{linearData}. Its fields are the equation list \texttt{linearEquations}, the master-integral
ordering \texttt{integralList}, the pivot rules \texttt{integralRules}, the sector metadata
\texttt{sectorMetadataList} and the readiness reports. The order of \texttt{integralList} fixes the
master-integral ordering. The command \texttt{DSReorderIntegrals} re-indexes it if
the user prefers a different order. The two-stage Kira plan is built purely in memory:
\begin{verbatim}
prePlan   = DSKiraPlan[linearData, <|"stage" -> "preReduction", ...|>];
preExport = DSKiraExport[prePlan];
(* ... external Kira run identifies the master integrals ... *)
userMIData = DSUserMI[linearData, userExpressions, <|
   "names" -> activeNames, "activeIndices" -> activeIndices,
   "derivativeVariables" -> {ss11, P0}, "scalingDegrees" -> degrees|>];
formalPlan   = DSKiraPlan[linearWithUserMI, <|"stage" -> "formal", ...|>];
formalExport = DSKiraExport[formalPlan];
(* ... external Kira run reduces to the user basis ... *)
importedData = DSKiraImport[formalWorkspace];
\end{verbatim}
The pre-reduction stage freezes the candidate targets and lets the external reducer discover the
masters. The command \texttt{DSUserMI} then attaches the user basis. It checks the global and the
active rank, and it stores reversible pivot rules. The formal stage constructs the analytic
derivative closure of the active basis \emph{before} any export. It refuses to write if internal
helper symbols survive. Export manifests carry the SHA-256 identity of every artifact, and
\texttt{DSKiraImport} re-verifies the contents instead of trusting version strings. Kira accepts only
real rational coefficients, so the serializer rewrites the momenta that must lie on the imaginary
axis by the backend rule \texttt{k -> -I ik}, where \texttt{I} is the imaginary unit and \texttt{ik}
is the backend symbol for the same magnitude. This convention is confined to the serializer and to
the importer, and the manifest stores the physical section, the map in both directions and the
resulting relation between the derivatives. The same \texttt{linearData}
feeds the Rational Tracer \cite{Magerya:2022hvj} pipeline through the equation-system interface.

\subsection{Differential equations and checks}
\label{sec:de}

After the reduction, \texttt{DSDE} differentiates the master integrals with respect to the
independent variables. Loop-Gram coordinates are handled through external-vector derivative
decompositions. No-loop magnitudes are handled through propagator-local radial derivatives, including
their dependent bindings. Vertex energies enter as scalar parameters. Explicit coefficients are
differentiated directly, while bare integrals are shifted and canonicalized. The result is reduced
back to the active basis. For the pure massive bubble the resulting equations are in $d\log$ form.
At the recorded exact point they reproduce the matrices of ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp} entry by entry.

Every integral of the family is a homogeneous function of the dimensionful variables. Let
$x_1,\dots,x_m$ denote the variables that a run keeps symbolic: the loop coordinates
$\texttt{ss}ij$, the no-loop magnitudes $\texttt{sE}i$ and the vertex energies $E_v$. The masses and
the space-time dimension enter the same identity only when they are kept symbolic as well, and the
released examples freeze them at a rational probe point. Each master integral $I_\alpha$ of the
active basis then satisfies the Euler
relation
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j=1}^{m} x_j\,\frac{\partial I_\alpha}{\partial x_j} \;=\; d_\alpha\, I_\alpha\,,
\label{eq:euler}
\end{equation}
where $d_\alpha$ is the constant scaling degree of $I_\alpha$. In matrix form, with
$\partial I/\partial x_j=\Omega_j(x)\,I$ for the vector of masters, the identity \eqref{eq:euler}
becomes
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j=1}^{m} x_j\, \Omega_j(x) \;=\; \Delta\,,\qquad
\Delta=\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_R)\,,
\label{eq:eulerMatrix}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the number of masters in the active basis.
The command \texttt{DSScaleCheck} verifies \eqref{eq:eulerMatrix} symbolically on the reduced basis.
It also checks the source-level time and loop-momentum IBP identities before the reduction. Because
$\Delta$ is constant and diagonal, \eqref{eq:eulerMatrix} is a strong self-consistency test of the
whole chain from seeds to differential equations. The tree-level track exposes
\texttt{DSTreeDLogDE} for direct $d\log$ differential equations.

At the atomic level the package also exposes the generators themselves:
\begin{verbatim}
dtau[i, expr, context]     (* time generator at vertex i *)
dqq[i, j, expr, context]   (* d/dq_i . q_j *)
dqk[i, j, expr, context]   (* d/dq_i . k_j *)
ds[expr, var, context]     (* full variable derivative *)
\end{verbatim}
together with the form rewriters \texttt{rep2innerform}, \texttt{rep2outform} and
\texttt{rep2Integrand}. In these four calls the first argument of \texttt{dtau} is a vertex index,
and the first two arguments of \texttt{dqq} and \texttt{dqk} are loop-momentum and external-momentum
indices. The last command expands a $J$ into its explicit integrand without
integrating. The short signatures rely on the single registered topology. Explicit contexts should
be used when several topologies are alive.

\subsection{Tree-level track}
\label{sec:tree}

The tree-level functions mirror the loop workflow:
\begin{verbatim}
treeSeeds = DSTreeSeeds[context];
iterative = repIterative[treeSeeds, context];
naiveIBP  = DSTreeNaiveIBP[treeSeeds, context];
naiveDE   = DSTreeNaiveDE[naiveIBP, context];
dlogDE    = DSTreeDLogDE[iterative, context];
\end{verbatim}
The iterative route substitutes the general derived formulas directly. The naive route reduces raw
sampled IBP relations. The two routes must agree, and the package reports the flag
\texttt{deRoutesAgree}. The tree-level differential equations have also been compared with the
package MadStree \cite{MadStree:inpreparation}. MadStree evaluates tree-level cosmological
correlators and builds its own $d\log$ differential equations. For a mixed three-vertex tree family
with fifteen masters, the parameter derivatives
of \texttt{dSIBP} were reduced by MadStree for the two no-loop magnitudes
$\texttt{sE}1,\texttt{sE}2$ and the three vertex energies of the family. All seventy-five
derivatives closed, and the five
$15\times15$ matrices agree entry by entry with the direct $d\log$ formula route of MadStree.
Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} summarizes the two tracks and their connection to
the external reducers. Appendix~\ref{app:api} lists the public functions with short descriptions.

\section{Examples}
\label{sec:examples}

The package ships six worked examples. Figure~\ref{fig:examples} shows the four graphs discussed
below. The quoted Kira times are wall-clock times of the external reduction on a single
workstation. The Kira inputs were written by \texttt{DSKiraExport}.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{figures/fig_examples.pdf}
\caption{Example graphs. The fill colour of a vertex encodes its branch sign: black is $+$ and
white is $-$. (a)~The two-loop sunrise of example~03: one massive full propagator of mass $\nu_1$
and two massless full propagators between two $+$ vertices; both vertices declare the same
vertex energy, written \texttt{kE}. (b)~The pure massive bubble of example~04: two massive full
propagators
of the same mass $\nu$ between two $-$ vertices with vertex energy $P_0$; the loop external momentum
is $k$. (c)~The mixed bubble-plus-tree case of example~06: a massive full propagator of mass
$\nu_1$ and a massless full propagator form the loop between $v_1$ and $v_2$, and a massless fixed
propagator, a bridge, connects $v_2$ and $v_3$; the external legs carry $k_1+k_2$, $k_1$ and $k_2$.
(d)~The
two-vertex tree of example~05: a massive propagator of mass $\nu_{12}$ whose momentum $p_{12}$ is
a no-loop magnitude.}
\label{fig:examples}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Sunrise (example 03).}
The graph of figure~\ref{fig:examples}a has two vertices joined by three parallel propagators,
hence $L=2$. Propagator~1 is a massive full propagator of mass $\nu_1$, and propagators~2 and~3
are massless full propagators. The loop
momenta are $l_3$ and $k_{321}$, and the single loop external momentum is $k_L$. Thus $K=1$, and
the loop Gram matrix gives the coordinate $\texttt{ss}11=|k_L|$. No no-loop magnitude is declared.
Both vertices declare the same vertex energy, written $\mathtt{kE}$ in the case, and this is the
second derivative variable.
The top sector receives two time generators and $L(L+K)=6$ loop-momentum generators. Two ISPs are
declared, $\mathrm{sp}[k_{321},l_3]$ and $\mathrm{sp}[l_3-k_{321}-k_L,l_3]$, each with range
$\{0,1\}$. The symmetry that swaps the two massless propagators exchanges them, and the package
canonicalizes the two ISPs together with that swap. The example stops after the general stage. It
generates the canonical seeds and the general parameter-derivative operators, and it neither
samples the continuous labels nor runs a reduction, a differential equation or a scaling check. The
seeds are checked by applying \texttt{ds} to the top-sector integral with respect to both
variables.

\paragraph{Pure massive bubble (example 04).}
The bubble of figure~\ref{fig:examples}b has two vertices on the $-$ branch. Its two massive full
propagators carry the same mass $\nu$ and the loop momenta $q$ and $q-k$. The loop external
momentum $k$
gives the loop coordinate $\texttt{ss}11=|k|$. Both vertex energies are set to the parameter
$P_0$, which is the second derivative variable. This example runs the full closed loop: seeds,
envelope sweep, linear system, two-stage Kira export, external reduction, import, differential
equations and scaling check. The Kira workspace must lie outside the repository, and the package
itself never starts Kira. The linear system is written at a fixed rational probe point for the
dimension and the masses, while the differential equations keep $\texttt{ss}11$ and $P_0$ symbolic.
The reduction closes with no unreduced target and returns nineteen master integrals. The two
$19\times19$ differential-equation matrices in $d\log$ form reproduce those of
ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp}: at the recorded exact point each of the three variable conventions agrees
entry by entry, $361/361$. The scaling identities \eqref{eq:eulerMatrix} hold symbolically on the
same basis, with $53/53$ source-level identities and $19/19$ active-basis checks.

\paragraph{Mixed bubble plus tree (example 06).}
The most demanding released case is figure~\ref{fig:examples}c. Propagators~1 and~2 form the loop:
propagator~1 is a massive full propagator of mass $\nu_1$, and propagator~2 is a massless full
propagator. Propagator~3 is a massless fixed propagator, a bridge, between $v_2$ and $v_3$. It lies
outside the loop, so
its pack carries no
denominator power and its magnitude power sits in $N_s$. One external leg enters $v_1$ and carries
$k_1+k_2$, and two external legs enter $v_3$ and carry $k_1$ and $k_2$. The vertex $v_2$ has no
external leg. The declaration gives the three vertices the three independent energies
$E_1,E_2,E_3$ of \eqref{eq:vertexPhase}, and these are derivative variables in their own right. The loop
external momentum is $k_1+k_2$, which gives $\texttt{ss}11$. The independent no-loop momenta
$k_1,k_2$ give the magnitudes $\texttt{sE}1,\texttt{sE}2$. Both cycle propagators are restricted to
even parity,
$b_e+n_{e,1}+n_{e,2}\equiv0$ for $e=1,2$ in the labels of \eqref{eq:parity}, while the
fixed propagator is excluded from the
parity constraints. The parity-restricted family has eighty-one master integrals, and the generated
system contains 818{,}217 relations. The external Kira reduction takes about 160\,s and closes with
no unreduced target. The package then produces six $81\times81$ differential-equation matrices, one
for each of the variables $\texttt{ss}11,\texttt{sE}1,\texttt{sE}2,E_1,E_2,E_3$, with no residual
symbols. All $81/81$ source-level Euler and IBP identities vanish exactly. The reduction-based
scaling certificate is evaluated at the recorded exact point, because all six scaling variables are
fixed in the formal plan. The example also exercises the parameter redefinition
\texttt{DSRedefineParameters}. One redefinition replaces the two separate magnitudes of $k_1$ and
$k_2$ by a bound energy $E_0=|k_1|+|k_2|$, through the rules
$\texttt{sp[k1,k1] -> (E0-sE2)\textasciicircum2}$ and $\texttt{sp[k2,k2] -> sE2\textasciicircum2}$,
and it sets the energy of $v_3$ to $E_0$. Another redefinition routes a single leg of momentum $p_0$
into $v_3$ with $\texttt{sp[p0,p0] -> E0\textasciicircum2}$. The example also runs the
over-complete and under-complete kinematic audits.

\paragraph{Two-vertex tree (example 05).}
A massive propagator of mass $\nu_{12}$ connects two $+$ vertices, figure~\ref{fig:examples}d.
There is no loop momentum. The momentum $p_{12}$ of this propagator is declared as an independent
no-loop momentum, so the package builds the coordinate $\texttt{sE}1=|p_{12}|$. The
\texttt{timeOnly} family has the two sector keys \texttt{"1"} and \texttt{"0"}, for the full and
the shrunk propagator. The iterative route and the naive IBP route give the same $d\log$
differential equations in $E_1,E_2,\texttt{sE}1$, so \texttt{deRoutesAgree} is \texttt{True}. The
same example also declares a massless family with a single propagator.
Its seeds run over the two quotient representatives $F_{00}$ and $F_{10}$ of
\eqref{eq:masslessQuotient}, which the example records as the rule list
$n_{1,1}\to0$, $n_{1,2}\to0$ and $n_{1,1}\to1$ over the sector keys
\texttt{"0"} and \texttt{"1"}. Its formula route returns \texttt{PendingRederivation} with the
reason \texttt{masslessQuotientFormulaNotCertified}, as required by the gate of
section~\ref{sec:timeonly}.

\paragraph{Mixed bubble workflow and bare Hankel system (examples 01, 02).}
Example~01 declares a bubble with one massive and one massless full propagator between two $+$ vertices.
It runs the unified public entry through the discrete templates, the continuous sweep and the
linear system. It builds the pre-reduction plan in memory without exporting or running Kira.
Example~02 supplies a bare Hankel function system $(P,Q,T,W)$ instead of the \texttt{"h"} preset.
The package compiles it through the same public workflow and generates the seeds of the
corresponding family. Together the two examples cover the initialization, the envelope sweep and
the function-system interface of sections~\ref{sec:using} and~\ref{sec:dynamics}.

\section{Summary and outlook}
\label{sec:summary}

\texttt{dSIBP} turns the canonical IBP structure of de Sitter time integrals into a practical
\textsc{Mathematica} tool. From a graph declaration it produces sealed canonical seeds,
backend-neutral linear systems, Kira and Rational-Tracer export manifests with artifact identities,
differential equations and scaling checks. It accepts arbitrary topologies that mix massive and
massless propagators of all four Schwinger--Keldysh types. The parity-split structure, the massive
equation-of-motion reduction and the massless quotient are applied by construction, so the user
never handles raw label spaces. Ongoing work concerns the certification of the tree-level formulas
for families that contain massless full propagators, which are currently gated behind
\texttt{PendingRederivation}. Further
directions are a deeper integration with hypergeometric solution methods
\cite{Chen:2024glu,Fan:2024iek} and
the connection to numerical evaluation pipelines such as MadStree
\cite{MadStree:inpreparation} and the coupling of the reduced equations to numerical solvers for
Feynman-integral differential equations \cite{Hidding:2020ytt,Liu:2022chg}.
