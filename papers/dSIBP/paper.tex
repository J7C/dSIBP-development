\abstract{
Integration-by-parts (IBP) relations and differential equations provide a systematic approach to
Feynman integrals in de Sitter spacetime. \texttt{dSIBP} is a \textsc{Mathematica} package that
makes this approach available from a graph declaration. For families with massive and massless
propagators, it generates canonical IBP seeds, assembles linear systems for external reduction,
and constructs differential equations for the reduced master integrals. The implementation
combines equations of motion, contact terms and parity constraints, and provides Euler scaling
checks. A complementary tree-level workflow implements iterative reduction and direct $d\log$
differential equations for supported time-only families. We describe the conventions and public
interface through worked examples, including a massive bubble and a mixed loop-plus-tree family
with eighty-one master integrals.
}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Correlation functions in de Sitter (dS) spacetime describe the quantum fluctuations relevant to
inflation. In the Schwinger--Keldysh formulation, their Feynman graphs involve integrations over
vertex times and, at loop level, spatial loop momenta \cite{Chen:2017ryl}. Massive propagators
introduce Hankel functions into these integrals. Generalised integration-by-parts (IBP) relations
and differential equations (DE) offer a systematic way to organise this calculation
\cite{Chen:2023iix,Chen:2024glu,Chen:2026dqp}.

In flat-spacetime QFT, IBP relations reduce integral families to master integrals
\cite{Chetyrkin:1981qh,Laporta:2000dsw}. Differentiating these masters and reducing the result gives
closed differential equations in the kinematic variables
\cite{Kotikov:1990kg,Kotikov:1991pm,Bern:1993kr,Gehrmann:1999as}.
A suitable basis can simplify the equations, in particular to canonical $d\log$ form
\cite{Henn:2013pwa}. This approach is supported by reduction programs such as FIRE
\cite{Smirnov:2019qkx,Smirnov:2025prc} and Kira
\cite{Maierhofer:2017gsa,Klappert:2020nbg}, rational-function reconstruction tools such as
Rational Tracer \cite{Magerya:2022hvj}, and numerical DE solvers such as DiffExp
\cite{Hidding:2020ytt}. The auxiliary mass flow method and its implementation AMFlow provide
another example of turning reduction and differential equations into an automated numerical
calculation \cite{Liu:2017jxz,Liu:2022chg,Liu:2022mfb}.

For dS integrals, ref.~\cite{Chen:2023iix} derives general IBP reduction formulas and $d\log$
differential equations for vertex integral families with Hankel functions.
Reference~\cite{Chen:2024glu} develops power-series solutions, multivariate hypergeometric
representations and boundary conditions for vertex integral families and the homogeneous parts
of tree integrals.
At loop level, ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp} combines time and momentum IBP relations and organises
the system into subsystems labelled by the parities of propagator indices. Its massive bubble
example illustrates the reduction to master integrals and the construction of $d\log$
differential equations. Together these results provide the basis for a systematic computational
approach to dS integrals. Applying it to a graph requires consistent treatment of the endpoint
states of each propagator, the contact terms from time ordering, and the kinematic derivatives.

\texttt{dSIBP} automates this construction. Starting from vertices, propagators and momentum
assignments, it builds an integral family and generates canonical IBP seeds. Equations of motion
reduce massive endpoint states, algebraic relations identify massless states, and parity
constraints restrict the generated system. The user chooses the ranges of integral indices and
passes the resulting linear system to an external reducer. After importing a reduction, the
package constructs differential equations for a chosen master basis and checks Euler scaling
identities.

For loop families, the linear
systems are solved externally; \texttt{dSIBP} does not launch Kira or provide a numerical solver
for the resulting DE. A separate time-only workflow implements internal iterative reduction and
direct $d\log$ equations for supported tree families. Its current scope is specified in
section~\ref{sec:timeonly}. Comparisons with analytic results and with MadStree
\cite{MadStree:inpreparation} test the implementation as described in
section~\ref{sec:validation}.

Section~\ref{sec:notation} introduces the integral representation and the conventions needed for
graph declarations. Section~\ref{sec:using} follows the calculation from initialisation to
differential equations. Section~\ref{sec:examples} presents the examples and validation results,
and section~\ref{sec:summary} discusses further work. Appendix~\ref{app:api} lists the public
functions.

\noindent\textbf{Statement on AI use.}
The author designed the notation, functionality, interfaces and validation tasks of \texttt{dSIBP}.
Several agent programs, each driven by a different large language model, implemented the code and
documentation under the author's supervision. The validation combines comparisons with analytic results and separately constructed
calculations with internal consistency checks. Section~\ref{sec:validation} distinguishes the
tests of IBP relations, differential equations and scaling identities.

\section{Notation and conventions}
\label{sec:notation}

A graph declaration fixes the integral family on which the IBP and derivative operators act.
This section specifies the propagator states, index conventions and kinematic variables needed
to interpret that family and its contact sectors.

\subsection{Schwinger--Keldysh Feynman rules}
\label{sec:skrules}

A de Sitter Feynman graph in the in-in formalism consists of vertices $v$ with conformal time
$\tau_v<0$ and Schwinger--Keldysh branch signs $\sigma_v=\pm$. Each vertex with external energy
$E_v$ carries the phase factor
\begin{equation}
e^{-\mathrm{i} \sigma_v E_v \tau_v}\,,
\label{eq:vertexPhase}
\end{equation}
and the time integrations run over the full interval $\tau_v\in(-\infty,0)$. An internal propagator
$e$ joining the ordered vertices $u$ and $v$ is one of four types. It is massive or massless, and it
is \emph{full} when the two branch signs agree, $\sigma_u=\sigma_v$, and \emph{cross} when they
differ, $\sigma_u=-\sigma_v$. These are the propagators $G_{\sigma\sigma}$ and
$G_{\sigma,-\sigma}$ of refs.~\cite{Chen:2023iix,Chen:2024glu,Chen:2026dqp}. Only a full propagator
contains a Heaviside ordering of the two vertex
times, so only a full propagator produces $\theta$-shrink terms. The assignments $++$ and $--$
belong to the same full class, and $+-$ and $-+$ to the same cross class. Their kernels retain
the branch dependence; equality of classes does not imply equality of propagators.
In our figures a circle denotes a vertex, and
its fill colour encodes the branch sign: black is $\sigma_v=+$, white is $\sigma_v=-$, and grey
leaves the sign general. The conformal time $\tau_v$ is written below the
vertex and the vertex energy beside it. External legs end in small open squares. Each vertex carries
one energy $E_v$, the energy that appears in its phase factor. It is a symbol of the declaration: the
user may bind it to the packaged magnitudes of the external legs attached to $v$, or keep it
independent even when no external leg is attached to it. Figure~\ref{fig:skrules} shows these
rules for the bubble input of the
package. Its two vertices are drawn in grey because their branch signs are general. Vertex~1
carries the external momenta $k_{1,1},\dots,k_{1,n_1}$ and vertex~2 carries
$k_{2,1},\dots,k_{2,n_2}$. The internal momenta are $q$ and $q-k$.

The vertex energies $E_1,E_2$ and the conformal times $\tau_1,\tau_2$ are written at the vertices.

\label{fig:skrules}
\end{figure}

The package distinguishes the four propagator types as
$\langle$\texttt{massive, massless}$\rangle\times\langle$\texttt{full, cross}$\rangle$.
Figure~\ref{fig:linetypes} shows the resulting graphical conventions. Every propagator that lies
on a cycle becomes a denominator of the integral family; the propagators on cycles form the
\emph{cycle skeleton} of the graph. A propagator that does not lie on a cycle
is a \emph{fixed propagator}, which the package also calls a \emph{bridge}. It carries
only a magnitude power and no loop-momentum denominator. Massive propagators whose two vertices
become coincident under a contact operation
may be \emph{shrunk}. A shrink removes the time-dependent block and merges its endpoints;
its residual magnitude powers and normalisation are retained. A fixed propagator retains its
block until it is shrunk, while its magnitude power belongs to the sector normalisation $N_s$
defined in section~\ref{sec:family}.

encodes its branch sign: black is $+$ and white is $-$. The conjugate assignments $--$ and $-+$

$\{b_e,n_{e,1},n_{e,2}\}$, with the massive vertex states restricted to $\{0,1\}$. A massless cross

\subsection{The integral family}
\label{sec:family}

A sector $s$ specifies which propagator blocks remain and which have been shrunk by contact
terms. Its active vertices are the vertices remaining after the corresponding identifications.
Suppressing constant integration-measure conventions, a family member has the form
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
J_s={}&N_s\!\int\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}d^dq_\ell
\int_{-\infty}^{0}\prod_{v\in V_s}d\tau_v\;
\prod_{v\in V_s}(-\tau_v)^{A_v}e^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma_v E_v\tau_v}\\
&\times\prod_{e\in\mathcal C}\xi_e^{-B_e}
\prod_{e\in U_s}\mathcal B_e^{(n_{e,1},n_{e,2})}
(\xi_e;\tau_u,\tau_v)\;
\prod_r\rho_r^{\,i_r}\,.
\end{split}
\label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
Here $d$ is the spatial dimension, $q_\ell$ are loop momenta, and $V_s$ is the set of active
vertices. The set $\mathcal C$ contains the cycle propagators of the declared graph, with momentum
magnitudes $\xi_e$. The set $U_s$ contains the unshrunk propagators, including fixed propagators.
For $e=(u,v)$, $\mathcal B_e^{(n_{e,1},n_{e,2})}$ is the corresponding Schwinger--Keldysh block.
Its superscripts label endpoint states, not powers of the block: for a massive propagator,
states $0$ and $1$ select a mode function and its first derivative with respect to its argument.
Massless states are specified below. The ISPs $\rho_r$ and their integer powers $i_r$ are defined
in section~\ref{sec:kinematics}.

The time powers are $A_v=a_v+a0_v$. For an unshrunk cycle propagator, the momentum denominator
power is $B_e=b_e+b0_e$; after a shrink it is the corresponding sector power
$b_{S,e}+b_{S0,e}$. The zero points $a0_v$, $b0_e$ and their child-sector values are part of the
declaration and its contact rules. The factor $N_s$ collects the powers of no-loop magnitudes
and the normalisation constants associated with contractions. Fixed propagators contribute
magnitude powers through this factor. Residual cycle-momentum powers remain under the loop
integral. Kinematic derivatives act on $N_s$ as well as on the remaining integrand.
In the package the family is written in the three-slot schema
\begin{verbatim}
J[aList, linePacks, ispList]
\end{verbatim}
The first slot is the list of vertex time powers $a_v$ of the vertices that are still distinct in
this sector, the \emph{active} vertices of the sector, in the frozen vertex order. The second slot
holds one pack per propagator, in the order
in which the declaration key \texttt{"lines"} lists them, and the shapes of the packs identify the
sector. The third slot lists the ISPs with their integer powers $i_r$. A case is one named
declaration. Its keys give the vertices with their branch signs, the propagators with their
endpoints, momenta and mass types, the external legs, the loop momenta, and the two lists of
external momenta. They also carry the zero points, the symmetry rules and the parity constraints
discussed below.

\begin{itemize}
\item A cyclic massive full, massive cross or massless full propagator carries
$\{b_e,n_{e,1},n_{e,2}\}$: the power $b_e$ and the two state powers. The three integers are the
propagator indices of refs.~\cite{Chen:2023iix,Chen:2024glu,Chen:2026dqp}; in this paper they are
called the propagator power and the state powers. The latter name denotes endpoint-state
indices, not ordinary powers of a function.
\item A cyclic massless cross propagator contributes $\{b_e,0,0\}$. It is a single exponential
product, so it has no discrete state.
\item A fixed propagator contributes $\{\texttt{"F"},n_{e,1},n_{e,2}\}$: no propagator power, and
its magnitude power is collected in $N_s$.
\item A shrunk propagator contributes $\{b_{S,e}\}$, or $\{\texttt{"F"}\}$ when it was already
fixed. The cycle-momentum power remains in the loop integrand; no-loop magnitude powers are
collected in $N_s$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Massive vertex states and equations of motion.}
Every massive propagator is assigned a second-order function system
$f''+P(x)f'+Q(x)f=0$. Its variable is $x=-\xi\tau$, the negative of the product of the momentum
magnitude $\xi$ of the propagator and the vertex time $\tau$, and primes are derivatives with respect to
$x$. Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} lists the presets. A massive propagator $e$ with mass parameter
$\nu_e$
and the \texttt{"h"} preset $P_{\mathrm h}(x)=(2\nu_e+1)/x$,
$Q_{\mathrm h}(x)=1$ has Hankel blocks at each of its two vertices that span a two-dimensional space.
The space is
indexed by the state $n_{e,i}$, where $i=1,2$ names the endpoint of $e$, and $a_{e,i}$ denotes the
time power of that same endpoint. For a cycle propagator, the second-order equation of motion
at $n_{e,i}=2$ gives the exact relation
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
J\big[\cdots,n_{e,i}=2,\cdots\big]
={}&-J\big[\cdots,n_{e,i}=0,\cdots\big]\\
&-(2\nu_e+1)\,J\big[\cdots,n_{e,i}=1,\,a_{e,i}\!\to\!a_{e,i}\!-\!1,\,b_e\!\to\!b_e\!+\!1,\cdots\big],
\end{split}
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
so every massive vertex state reduces to the representative set $\{0,1\}$. For a fixed
propagator the second term instead carries an explicit factor $\xi_e^{-1}$ and only shifts
$a_{e,i}\to a_{e,i}-1$; there is no $b_e$ label to shift.
The package applies this reduction by construction: the generated seeds contain only
$n_{e,i}\in\{0,1\}$.

The zero points belong to the declaration. A case supplies them through the key
\texttt{zeroPointRules}, as rules for $a0_v$ and $b0_e$. Two settings of this key are used by the
released examples. The sunrise and the mixed case keep them as free regulator symbols, for example
$a0_v=\alpha_v$ and $b0_e=\beta_e$. The pure massive bubble fixes them instead: for a propagator of
mass parameter $\nu$ under the \texttt{"h"} preset it takes
$b0_e=-2\nu$, and at
the vertices it takes $a0_v=2\nu+\texttt{analyticRegulator}$. The last symbol is kept during seed
generation and set to zero afterwards. It is what the initialisation audit of
section~\ref{sec:init} asks for when the small-time leading powers of a family reach a divergent
time-integral boundary under integer index shifts. With
the zero points fixed this way, the labels $a_v,b_e,n_{e,i}$ that the package manipulates run over
small integers. They make the IBP shifts uniform: the generators shift the package-level labels by
$\pm1$ and never touch the zero points.

\paragraph{Massless quotient.}
A massless full propagator $e$ between the ordered vertices $u$ and $v$ has two independent
states. Let $\Delta=\tau_u-\tau_v$ and let $q$ be the magnitude of its momentum. Its kernel is
built from the two functions
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
M_0={}&\theta(\Delta)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta}
      +\theta(-\Delta)\,e^{+\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta},\\
M_1={}&-\theta(\Delta)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta}
      +\theta(-\Delta)\,e^{+\mathrm{i}\sigma q\Delta},
\end{split}
\label{eq:masslessBlocks}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the common branch sign of the two endpoints, so $\sigma=+1$ on $++$ and
$\sigma=-1$ on $--$. Exchanging the two vertices leaves $M_0$ unchanged and flips the sign of $M_1$.
Write $F_{n_1n_2}$ for its propagator block with
$(n_{e,1},n_{e,2})=(n_1,n_2)$. The package works with the quotient
\begin{equation}
F_{00}=M_0,\qquad F_{10}=M_1,\qquad
F_{01}=-F_{10},\qquad F_{11}=-F_{00},
\label{eq:masslessQuotient}
\end{equation}
so only the two representatives $F_{00}$ and $F_{10}$ are independent. The pack of such a
propagator still
keeps both state powers, and each of them takes the values $0$ and $1$. Seeds are generated for the two
representatives only, and a state produced by a derivative is reduced back with $n_{e,2}\to0$. This is
an exact algebraic identification, not a numerical sampling rule.

\paragraph{Contact terms and sector normalisation.}
Differentiating the time-ordering factors produces contact terms, also called pinching or
shrinking terms in refs.~\cite{Chen:2023iix,Chen:2024glu,Chen:2026dqp}. For the default massive
and massless full blocks, a nonzero elementary contact requires $n_{e,1}+n_{e,2}=1$.
The package merges the endpoints, removes the corresponding block, and maps the time powers,
momentum powers and zero points to the child sector. For a cycle propagator in the default
\texttt{"h"} system, for example, the merged time label is $a_u+a_v-1$, its zero point is
$a0_u+a0_v-2\nu_e$, and the residual momentum labels are $b_{S,e}=b_e+1$ and
$b_{S0,e}=b0_e+2\nu_e$. For a massless contact the time powers add without these shifts, and
$b_{S,e}=b_e$.

Parallel full propagators joining the same pair of active vertices share a time-ordering
boundary. They must be differentiated as one product. For each such bundle $B$, the contact
expansion contains nonempty odd subsets $Q\subseteq B$ with combinatorial factor $2^{1-|Q|}$.
Each term merges the endpoint pair once. If $c^{\mathrm{raw}}_{sQ}$ is its coefficient in the
unnormalised integral convention, including the combinatorial and elementary contact factors,
then its contribution in the public convention is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\substack{\emptyset\neq Q\subseteq B\\ |Q|\ \mathrm{odd}}}
c^{\mathrm{raw}}_{sQ}\,\frac{N_s}{N_{t_Q}}\,J_{t_Q}\,.
\label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
Here $t_Q$ denotes the child sector with all index and zero-point shifts applied. The ratio of
normalisations is essential: the child normalisation already contains the assigned contraction
constants and no-loop magnitude powers. The package applies this conversion when assembling
IBP relations, so these factors must not be multiplied into the public integrals a second time.

\subsection{Kinematics}
\label{sec:kinematics}

The structural loop number $L$ is the first Betti number of the internal undirected multigraph.
Let $N_E$ count the internal propagators, each one counted once: parallel propagators between the
same pair of vertices therefore contribute separately, and a self-loop contributes one.
External legs do not contribute to $N_E$. Let $N_V$ count the vertices and $C$
the connected components. Then
\begin{equation}
L=N_E-N_V+C\,.
\label{eq:loopnumber}
\end{equation}
Together with $K$ independent
loop-related external momenta, the loop number fixes the number of scalar products,
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{sp}}=\frac{L(L+1)}{2}+LK\,.
\label{eq:nsp}
\end{equation}
The user
declares two separate lists. The first list, \texttt{loopExternalMomenta} $\{k_1,\dots,k_K\}$,
defines the loop coordinates $\texttt{ss}ij=\sqrt{\mathrm{sp}[k_i,k_j]}$ of the loop Gram matrix.
The second list, \texttt{independentExternalMomenta}, defines the no-loop magnitudes
$|\texttt{kE}i|$ that appear in vertex energies and in the masses of fixed propagators. These
magnitudes are
parameterised by $\texttt{sE}i$. Only
magnitudes are coordinates. The package never outputs $\mathrm{sp}[k_{Ei},k_{Ej}]$, and it never
eliminates intermediate magnitudes by a cosine rule. Magnitudes that depend on the declared ones
are stored as explicit bindings. They still contribute to vertex phases through the chain rule.

User-defined ISPs are declared as \texttt{name/expr/range} associations, for example
\begin{verbatim}
<|"name" -> rho1, "expr" -> sp[q1, k], "range" -> {0, 1}|>,
\end{verbatim}
and they must form an invertible coordinate system on the space of scalar products together with
the propagator squares. The definition zero point of an ISP is fixed at $0$: positive powers are
numerator powers, and a negative power means an extra denominator.

The kinematic coordinates of a case are fixed by rules that express the basic scalar products in
terms of chosen symbols. A rule has the form
\texttt{sp[k1,k1] -> u\textasciicircum2}, and a rule can bind a whole energy as well, as
\texttt{sp[k1,k1] -> (E0-sE2)\textasciicircum2} does in the mixed example of
section~\ref{sec:examples}. The set of these rules is the kinematic system of the case, and its
symbols are exactly the variables that later derivatives and scaling checks use. The two-stage
interface \texttt{DSKinematics}/\texttt{DSInit}
validates this system and reports an exact, over-complete or under-complete
state. Only the exact state opens derivative generation.

\subsection{Parity structure}
\label{sec:parity}

For the massive \texttt{"h"} and \texttt{"H"} presets and the massless systems, the discrete
invariant of the dS IBP system is the parity of $b_e+n_{e,1}+n_{e,2}$ on a propagator.
The package does not automatically extend this parity support to general custom function systems.
For every propagator $e$ that carries a propagator power
$b_e$, that is for every propagator of the
cycle skeleton, the quantity
\begin{equation}
p_e \;=\; b_e+n_{e,1}+n_{e,2} \pmod 2
\label{eq:parity}
\end{equation}
is conserved by every time generator and every loop-momentum generator. A massless cross propagator has
$n_{e,1}=n_{e,2}=0$, so its parity is just $b_e$. A fixed propagator carries no propagator power, so it
enters no parity label, and the package rejects a constraint that names one. The parity vector
$(p_e)_e$ of the top sector labels the closed subsystems of the IBP system of a family, and for a
graph whose propagators all lie on the cycle skeleton this is exactly the split stated by
ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp}.

The default \texttt{"h"}
and \texttt{"H"} shrinks of a massive propagator, defined in section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, leave the parity
unchanged: such a shrink raises the power to $b_e+1$ and removes the single state power it consumes. A
massless contact keeps the power at $b_e$, so it offsets the parity of the child sector by one, and
several contacts accumulate modulo two. The package therefore does not apply a constraint to a sector
below the top sector in its original form. It rewrites the constraint in the powers of that sector,
using the power and zero-point shifts of its shrunk propagators. When the rewrite is not integral, the
parity filter is disabled.

For families with
user-provided \texttt{parityConstraints} the package keeps only the seeds whose transported constraint
vanishes. A constraint is written in the powers of \eqref{eq:parity}, for example
$b_1+n_{1,1}+n_{1,2}\equiv0$ for the propagator with declaration index $1$; the declaration spells
the same powers as \texttt{b[1]}, \texttt{n[1,1]} and \texttt{n[1,2]}. ISP indices receive no
parity filter.

\subsection{Massive function systems}
\label{sec:dynamics}

Besides the default \texttt{"h"} system defined in section~\ref{sec:family}, the package accepts
bare Hankel functions, the preset \texttt{"H"}, and
general user systems $(P,Q,T,W)$ whose coefficients admit the finite Laurent-shift representation
used by the generators and whose Wronskian data are consistent.
Let $T$ be an invertible $2\times2$ basis transformation acting on $(f,f')^{\mathsf T}$,
and let $W$ be the Wronskian before that transformation. The package
tracks the combination $W_T=\det(T)\,W$ through all shrinks. The $\theta$-coincidence and Wronskian
shrink terms used by the time-IBP generators are derived from it. Ordinary derivatives of the
kinematic coordinates never generate $\theta$ shrinks and never invoke $W_T$. The user chooses the
convention
once at initialisation. The IBP generators, the derivative operators and the tree-level track all
read the same compiled function system.

\subsection{Tree-level and time-only families}
\label{sec:timeonly}

For graphs without cycles the package exposes a \texttt{timeOnly} family with the unique public
representation
\begin{verbatim}
J[sectorKey_String, timeShifts_List, stateBits_List]
\end{verbatim}
Here the \emph{root topology} is the declared graph before any contact, and its propagator order is
fixed for the whole family. The \texttt{sectorKey} records, for each propagator of the root
topology, whether it is full (1)
or shrunk (0). The list \texttt{timeShifts} gives the time powers $a_v$ of the active vertices. The
list \texttt{stateBits} gives one bit for each massive vertex state or massless quotient state, in
the fixed order of the root propagators. There are no momentum generators, no propagator powers
and no ISPs. The magnitude powers of fixed propagators remain in $N_s$
and still enter derivative generation. This mode is the default for acyclic graphs. It can also
be selected explicitly for the time-integral part of a graph with cycles, with its internal
momentum magnitudes treated as parameters rather than integration variables. In the full loop
mode, a non-cyclic propagator remains a fixed propagator in the three-slot family of
section~\ref{sec:family}. The direct tree-formula interfaces currently support massive-only families.
For a context containing any massless propagator, they return \texttt{PendingRederivation}
with reason \texttt{masslessQuotientFormulaNotCertified}. The general
\texttt{DSSeeds}, \texttt{DSGenerateIBP} and \texttt{DSLinear} route remains available for these
families.

\section{Using the package}
\label{sec:using}

The loop workflow has three stages: construct the family and its IBP system, obtain an external
reduction, and use it to build differential equations. The commands below illustrate these
stages; complete runnable declarations and scripts are supplied with the examples in
section~\ref{sec:examples}. Load the distributed \texttt{dSIBP.m} with
\texttt{Get[packageFile]}, where \texttt{packageFile} is its full path. The
\texttt{main.wl} script of example~04 supplies the massive-bubble declaration used below;
\texttt{case} denotes the input association and \texttt{custom} its kinematic rules.

\subsection{Initialisation}
\label{sec:init}

A case is the named declaration described in section~\ref{sec:family}. Initialisation runs a
topology audit of the loop number,
the registry of vertices and propagators and the external-momentum routing. It then runs the
kinematic audit of
section~\ref{sec:kinematics}. It also certifies the time-integration domain: if an integer shift of
the continuous labels drives the leading small-time powers of a vertex to a divergent boundary, the
run stops and names the vertices that need a symbolic regulator. The result is a context that every
later command consumes:
\begin{verbatim}
context = DSInit[case, KinematicRules -> custom];
\end{verbatim}
The optional two-stage interface \texttt{DSKinematics[case]} first returns a proposal with the
default rules and a selection template. The user can then inspect the kinematic coordinates
$\texttt{ss}ij,\texttt{sE}i$, the momentum-routing audit and the capability flags before committing.
Under-complete declarations abort the initialisation. Over-complete ones allow symbolic seed
generation but disable derivatives and the inner-form rewriting. In the over-complete case common
loop shifts are quotiented out through an affine basis \texttt{effectiveLoopExternalMomenta}.

\subsection{Seeds and the IBP index ranges}
\label{sec:seeds}

The sectors of a family are ordered by their contact operations. The \emph{top sector} retains
all propagator blocks; lower sectors arise by shrinking blocks and identifying their endpoints.
The labels of the top sector are the root labels used below.
\texttt{DSSeeds[context]} returns the canonical seed templates of the family, and
\texttt{DSAllSeeds} collects them into a list:
\begin{verbatim}
seedData = DSSeeds[context];
allSeeds = DSAllSeeds[seedData];
\end{verbatim}
Every sector
receives all the time generators of its active vertices and all $L(L+K)$ loop-momentum generators.
The massive vertex
states are pre-reduced by the equation of motion \eqref{eq:eom}. Massless full propagators run over
the two quotient representatives of \eqref{eq:masslessQuotient}. The discrete state powers are
eliminated, and the analytic seeds keep
general continuous labels $a_v,b_e,b_{S,e}$ and general ISP indices.
The canonicalisation applies the symmetry rules of the
declaration, where the user names a vertex swap or an exchange of parallel propagators, together with
built-in tadpole rules. A tadpole rule is available only for a self-loop whose reversal is a single
loop-momentum inversion; it either exchanges the two endpoint states of that propagator or maps the
integral to zero when an ISP carries an odd power.

The continuous labels are swept by \texttt{DSGenerateIBP}. The unified form
\begin{verbatim}
ibpData = DSGenerateIBP[allSeeds, {-1, 1}];
\end{verbatim}
requires that every root continuous index lies in the closed integer interval $[-1,1]$ \emph{after}
all shifts of the generated relations. The package inverts the per-index shift sets and derives the
narrower seed ranges automatically. The per-index form
\begin{verbatim}
ibpData = DSGenerateIBP[allSeeds,
   {a[v1], -1, 1}, {a[v2], 0, 1},
   {b[e1], 0, 2}, {b[e2], -1, 1}];
\end{verbatim}
must cover all root continuous indices exactly. ISP lower bounds are combined with the user target
by taking the larger lower bound, so the package never extends a range on its own in order to cover
numerator terms. Only complete systems may proceed to a formal reduction.

\subsection{Linear systems and external reduction}
\label{sec:linear}

The command
\begin{verbatim}
linearData = DSLinear[ibpData, context];
\end{verbatim}
converts the generated IBP relations into a backend-neutral association.
Its fields include the equations \texttt{linearEquations}, the full integral
list \texttt{integralList}, the integral-to-ID mapping \texttt{integralRules}, and sector metadata.
The command \texttt{DSReorderIntegrals} changes this ordering before export. Master integrals
are identified at the subsequent reduction stage. The two-stage Kira plan is built in memory:
\begin{samepage}
\begin{verbatim}
prePlan   = DSKiraPlan[linearData, <|"stage" -> "preReduction", ...|>];
preExport = DSKiraExport[prePlan];
(* ... external Kira run identifies the master integrals ... *)
linearWithUserMI = DSUserMI[linearData, userExpressions, <|
   "names" -> activeNames, "activeIndices" -> activeIndices,
   "derivativeVariables" -> {ss11, P0}, "scalingDegrees" -> degrees|>];
formalPlan   = DSKiraPlan[linearWithUserMI, <|"stage" -> "formal", ...|>];
formalExport = DSKiraExport[formalPlan];
(* ... external Kira run reduces to the user basis ... *)
importedData = DSKiraImport[formalWorkspace];
\end{verbatim}
\end{samepage}
The pre-reduction stage freezes the candidate targets and lets the external reducer discover the
masters. The command \texttt{DSUserMI} then attaches the user basis. It checks the global and the
active rank, and it stores reversible pivot rules. The formal stage constructs the analytic
derivative closure of the active basis \emph{before} any export.
\texttt{DSKiraImport} checks the export identity when importing the reduction. Kira accepts only
real rational coefficients, so the serialiser rewrites the momenta that must lie on the imaginary
axis by the backend rule \texttt{k -> -I ik}, where \texttt{I} is the imaginary unit and \texttt{ik}
is the backend symbol for the same magnitude. The importer reverses this map to restore the
physical variables and their derivative Jacobians. The current export/import interface is for Kira.
The backend-neutral \texttt{linearData} representation also provides the input needed to develop
serialisers for other reduction programs.

\subsection{Differential equations and checks}
\label{sec:de}

After the reduction, \texttt{DSDE} differentiates the master integrals with respect to the
independent variables:
\begin{verbatim}
deData = DSDE[importedData, {ss11, P0}];
\end{verbatim}
Loop-Gram coordinates are handled through external-vector derivative
decompositions. No-loop magnitudes are handled through propagator-local radial derivatives, including
their dependent bindings. Vertex energies enter as scalar parameters. Explicit coefficients are
differentiated directly, while bare integrals are shifted and canonicalised. The result is reduced
back to the chosen active basis. The pure massive bubble example supplies a known $d\log$ basis
from ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp}. Constructing the DE in that basis does not require a general
algorithm for finding a $d\log$ transformation.

Under a common rescaling of momentum magnitudes and vertex energies, each integral has a definite
degree. Let $x_1,\dots,x_m$ denote these dimension-one coordinates. The spatial dimension and the
mass parameters $\nu_e$ are held fixed under this rescaling; they may enter the scaling degrees.
For a basis of homogeneous master integrals, each $I_\alpha$ satisfies the Euler
relation
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j=1}^{m} x_j\,\frac{\partial I_\alpha}{\partial x_j} \;=\; d_\alpha\, I_\alpha\,,
\label{eq:euler}
\end{equation}
where $d_\alpha$ is the constant scaling degree of $I_\alpha$. In matrix form, with
$\partial I/\partial x_j=\Omega_j(x)\,I$ for the vector of masters, the identity \eqref{eq:euler}
becomes
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j=1}^{m} x_j\, \Omega_j(x) \;=\; \Delta\,,\qquad
\Delta=\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_R)\,,
\label{eq:eulerMatrix}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the number of masters in the active basis.
The command \texttt{DSScaleCheck} checks this matrix identity and any inhomogeneous source
terms in the DE output. It reports a symbolic check when the scaling variables remain symbolic,
a fixed-section check when some are fixed, and an exact-point check when all are fixed.
The source-level time and momentum IBP checks quoted in section~\ref{sec:examples} are separate
calculations in the validation scripts. The tree workflow exposes \texttt{DSTreeDLogDE} for
direct $d\log$ differential equations.

At the atomic level the package also exposes the generators themselves:
\begin{verbatim}
dtau[i, expr, context]     (* time generator at vertex i *)
dqq[i, j, expr, context]   (* d/dq_i . q_j *)
dqk[i, j, expr, context]   (* d/dq_i . k_j *)
ds[expr, var, context]     (* full variable derivative *)
\end{verbatim}
together with the form rewriters \texttt{rep2innerform}, \texttt{rep2outform} and
\texttt{rep2Integrand}. In these four calls the first argument of \texttt{dtau} is a vertex index,
and the first two arguments of \texttt{dqq} and \texttt{dqk} are loop-momentum and external-momentum
indices. The last command expands a $J$ into a formal integrand retaining named propagator blocks,
without integrating. The short signatures rely on the single registered topology. Explicit contexts should
be used when several topologies are registered.

\subsection{Tree-level track}
\label{sec:tree}

The tree-level functions mirror the loop workflow:
\begin{verbatim}
treeSeeds = DSTreeSeeds[integral, context];
dlogDE    = DSTreeDLogDE[context];
iterative = repIterative[target, end, context];
naiveIBP  = DSTreeNaiveIBP[context, masters];
naiveDE   = DSTreeNaiveDE[naiveIBP, variables];
\end{verbatim}
Here \texttt{integral} is a member of the \texttt{timeOnly} family, \texttt{masters} is the master
list of \texttt{dlogDE}, and \texttt{end} fixes the target time powers of the iterative reduction.
The iterative route substitutes the general reduction formulas, whereas the naive route solves
sampled IBP relations. The two-vertex example compares the naive DE with \texttt{DSTreeDLogDE}.
Section~\ref{sec:validation} describes this check and the separate comparison with MadStree.
Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} summarises the two workflows and their connection to
the external reducers. Table~\ref{tab:api} lists the public functions.

\section{Examples}
\label{sec:examples}

The package ships six worked examples. Figure~\ref{fig:examples} shows the four graphs discussed
below. The quoted Kira times are wall-clock times of the external reduction on a single
workstation. The Kira inputs were written by \texttt{DSKiraExport}.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{figures/fig_examples.pdf}
\caption{Example graphs. The fill colour of a vertex encodes its branch sign: black is $+$ and
white is $-$. (a)~The two-loop sunrise of example~03: one massive full propagator with mass
parameter $\nu_1$ and two massless full propagators between two $+$ vertices; both vertices declare
the same vertex energy, written \texttt{kE}. (b)~The pure massive bubble of example~04: two massive
full propagators with the same mass parameter $\nu$ between two $-$ vertices with vertex energy
$P_0$; the loop external momentum is $k$. (c)~The mixed bubble-plus-tree case of example~06: a
massive full propagator with mass parameter $\nu_1$ and a massless full propagator form the loop
between $v_1$ and $v_2$. A massless fixed propagator, a bridge, connects $v_2$ and $v_3$, and the
external legs carry $k_1+k_2$, $k_1$ and $k_2$. (d)~The
two-vertex tree of example~05: a massive propagator with mass parameter $\nu_{12}$ whose momentum
$p_{12}$ is a no-loop magnitude.}
\label{fig:examples}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Pure massive bubble (example 04).}
The bubble of figure~\ref{fig:examples}b has two vertices on the $-$ branch. Its two massive full
propagators carry the same mass parameter $\nu$ and the loop momenta $q$ and $q-k$. The loop external
momentum $k$
gives the loop coordinate $\texttt{ss}11=|k|$. Both vertex energies are set to the parameter
$P_0$, which is the second derivative variable. This example covers the full workflow: seeds,
index-range sweep, linear system, two-stage Kira export, external reduction, import, differential
equations and scaling check. The Kira workspace must lie outside the repository, and the package
itself never starts Kira. The linear system is written at a fixed rational probe point for the
dimension and the mass parameters, while the differential equations keep $\texttt{ss}11$ and $P_0$ symbolic.
The reduction terminates with no unreduced target and returns nineteen master integrals. The two
$19\times19$ differential-equation matrices in $d\log$ form agree with those of
ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp} at the recorded exact point. The scaling identities
\eqref{eq:eulerMatrix} hold symbolically on the same basis. Section~\ref{sec:validation}
gives the comparison conventions and check counts.

\paragraph{Mixed bubble plus tree (example 06).}
The largest released case is figure~\ref{fig:examples}c. Propagators~1 and~2 form the loop:
propagator~1 is a massive full propagator with mass parameter $\nu_1$, and propagator~2 is a
massless full propagator. Propagator~3 is a massless fixed propagator, a bridge, between $v_2$ and
$v_3$. It lies
outside the loop, so
its pack carries no propagator power and its magnitude power is collected in $N_s$. One external leg enters $v_1$ and carries
$k_1+k_2$, and two external legs enter $v_3$ and carry $k_1$ and $k_2$. The vertex $v_2$ has no
external leg. The declaration gives the three vertices the three independent energies
$E_1,E_2,E_3$ of \eqref{eq:vertexPhase}, and these are derivative variables in their own right. The loop
external momentum is $k_1+k_2$, which gives $\texttt{ss}11$. The independent no-loop momenta
$k_1,k_2$ give the magnitudes $\texttt{sE}1,\texttt{sE}2$. Both cycle propagators are restricted to
even parity,
$b_e+n_{e,1}+n_{e,2}\equiv0$ for $e=1,2$ in the labels of \eqref{eq:parity}, while the
fixed propagator is excluded from the
parity constraints. The parity-restricted family has eighty-one master integrals, and the generated
system contains 818{,}217 canonical relations. The formal Kira 2.3 reduction takes 160.11\,s
under WSL with ten threads and terminates with no unreduced target. This time excludes seed
generation, basis discovery, export and post-processing. At the recorded exact point, the package
produces six $81\times81$ connection-matrix values, one for each of
$\texttt{loopScale},\texttt{legScale1},\texttt{legScale2},E_1,E_2,E_3$.
The first three are the chosen names for the loop and two no-loop magnitude coordinates.
No unreduced integrals or backend helper symbols remain. All $81/81$ source-level Euler and
IBP identities vanish exactly. The reduction-based scaling certificate applies at that point:
all six scaling variables are fixed in the formal plan, so these results do not establish a
symbolic six-variable DE.

The example also illustrates how declarations and kinematic
redefinitions interact. One input assigns the energy $E_0$ to $v_3$ while retaining the two
external legs $k_1,k_2$. After \texttt{DSInit}, \texttt{DSRedefineParameters} imposes
$E_0=|k_1|+|k_2|$ through
$\texttt{sp[k1,k1] -> (E0-sE2)\textasciicircum2}$ and
$\texttt{sp[k2,k2] -> sE2\textasciicircum2}$.
A separate input replaces those two legs by one leg $p_0$ and assigns energy $E_0$ to $v_3$.
It is initialised as a new topology before applying
$\texttt{sp[p0,p0] -> E0\textasciicircum2}$. The example also runs the
over-complete and under-complete kinematic audits.

\paragraph{Sunrise (example 03).}
The graph of figure~\ref{fig:examples}a has two vertices joined by three parallel propagators,
hence $L=2$. Propagator~1 is a massive full propagator with mass parameter $\nu_1$, and
propagators~2 and~3
are massless full propagators. The loop
momenta are $l_3$ and $k_{321}$, and the single loop external momentum is $k_L$. Thus $K=1$, and
the loop Gram matrix gives the coordinate $\texttt{ss}11=|k_L|$. No no-loop magnitude is declared.
Both vertices declare the same vertex energy, written \texttt{kE} in the case, and this is the
second derivative variable.
The top sector receives two time generators and $L(L+K)=6$ loop-momentum generators. Two ISPs are
declared, $\mathrm{sp}[k_{321},l_3]$ and $\mathrm{sp}[l_3-k_{321}-k_L,l_3]$, each with range
$\{0,1\}$. The symmetry that swaps the two massless propagators exchanges them, and the package
canonicalises the two ISPs together with that swap. The example stops after the general stage. It
generates the canonical seeds and the general parameter-derivative operators, and it neither
samples the continuous labels nor runs a reduction, a differential equation or a scaling check. The
example demonstrates parameter derivative generation by applying \texttt{ds} to the top-sector
integral with respect to both variables.

\paragraph{Two-vertex tree (example 05).}
A massive propagator with mass parameter $\nu_{12}$ connects two $+$ vertices,
figure~\ref{fig:examples}d.
There is no loop momentum. The momentum $p_{12}$ of this propagator is declared as an independent
no-loop momentum, so the package builds the coordinate $\texttt{sE}1=|p_{12}|$. The
\texttt{timeOnly} family has the two sector keys \texttt{"1"} and \texttt{"0"}, for the full and
the shrunk propagator. The naive IBP route and the direct formula route give the same $d\log$
differential equations in $E_1,E_2,\texttt{sE}1$.
The example separately checks that \texttt{repIterative} returns without failure. The
same example also declares a massless family with a single propagator.
Its seeds are available over the two quotient representatives $F_{00}$ and $F_{10}$ of
\eqref{eq:masslessQuotient}, while its formula route remains pending as described in
section~\ref{sec:timeonly}.

\paragraph{Mixed bubble workflow and bare Hankel system (examples 01, 02).}
Example~01 declares a bubble with one massive and one massless full propagator between two $+$ vertices.
It runs the unified public entry through the discrete templates, the continuous sweep and the
linear system. It builds the pre-reduction plan in memory without exporting or running Kira.
Example~02 supplies a bare Hankel function system $(P,Q,T,W)$ instead of the \texttt{"h"} preset.
The package compiles it through the same public workflow and generates the seeds of the
corresponding family. Together the two examples cover the initialisation, the index-range sweep and
the function-system interface of sections~\ref{sec:using} and~\ref{sec:dynamics}.

\subsection{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

The checks below test different parts of the calculation. Comparisons with analytic reference
matrices test the differential equations at specified points; agreement there is not a symbolic
proof for all kinematics. Source-level identities and reduced-basis scaling checks provide
additional consistency tests.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Analytic tree reference.} For a massive two-vertex family, the general IBP seeds and
DE are compared with ref.~\cite{Chen:2024glu}, using its ordering of five normalised masters.
Each of the three $5\times5$ matrices agrees in all $25$ entries at the recorded probe point.
A separate comparison reduces sampled raw IBP relations of the same family and checks agreement
with the general formulas. These tests cover IBP relations and DE.
\item \emph{Loop reductions and scaling.} The pure massive bubble of example~04 combines an
external Kira reduction to nineteen masters with a comparison against the supplementary matrices
of arXiv v3 of ref.~\cite{Chen:2026dqp}. The benchmark directory of the package registers their
SHA-256 identity. The reference construction orients the energy oppositely,
$P_{\mathrm{ref}}=-P_0$. At the recorded exact point, all $361/361$ entries agree in each of three
derivative conventions: the physical $P_0$, the backend variable $\texttt{ip0}$ related by
$P_0=-\mathrm{i}\,\texttt{ip0}$, and $k_s=\texttt{ss}11$. The scaling identities
\eqref{eq:eulerMatrix} hold symbolically on the same basis, with $53/53$ source-level identities
and $19/19$ active-basis checks. The mixed bubble-plus-tree family of example~06 checks reduction
closure and the exact vanishing of all $81/81$ source-level Euler and IBP identities, and verifies
scaling on its eighty-one-master reduced basis at the recorded point. All six scaling variables
are fixed in its formal plan, so this certificate does not establish a symbolic six-variable DE.
These examples test the IBP-to-DE workflow and scaling identities. The mixed-family checks are
consistency tests, rather than a comparison with an independently known analytic solution.
\item \emph{Comparison with MadStree.} For a mixed three-vertex tree with fifteen masters,
\texttt{dSIBP} parameter derivatives are reduced using MadStree
\cite{MadStree:inpreparation}. The resulting matrices are compared with MadStree's direct
$d\log$ construction for two no-loop magnitudes and three vertex energies. All seventy-five
derivatives close, and the five $15\times15$ matrices agree entry by entry at the comparison
point. This checks the
\texttt{dSIBP} derivative operators against a separate DE construction; the reduction in this
test is performed by MadStree.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary and outlook}
\label{sec:summary}

\texttt{dSIBP} makes the dS IBP/DE framework accessible through graph declarations and a common
\textsc{Mathematica} interface. It automates the construction of integral families, canonical
seeds and linear systems, and uses externally obtained loop reductions to construct DE and
scaling checks. Equations of motion, massless state relations and parity constraints organise
the system before reduction. The examples demonstrate the workflow for massive and mixed
families, including a reduction to eighty-one masters.

The present implementation provides the algebraic input for subsequent evaluation of dS
integrals. Extending the direct tree formulas to families with massless propagators remains
an open implementation task. Further work concerns connecting the reduced DE to boundary-data
constructions and numerical evaluation, building on the analytic methods of
ref.~\cite{Chen:2024glu} and tools such as MadStree \cite{MadStree:inpreparation}.
