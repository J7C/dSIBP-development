1801: application is evaluated in the \texttt{NuConvention -> "Negative"} option, i.e.\ with
1802: $s_\nu=-1$ in the unified definition of Sec.~\ref{sec:notation}, because the physical mode
1803: functions and the printed formulae of \cite{Chen:2024glu} that we benchmark against are written in
1804: that option:
1805: \begin{equation}
1806:  \begin{aligned}
1807:  h^{(r)}(\nu,0;z)&=z^{-\nu}H^{(r)}_\nu(z),\\
1808:  h^{(r)}(\nu,1;z)&=\partial_z h^{(r)}(\nu,0;z)
1809:                   =-z^{-\nu}H^{(r)}_{\nu+1}(z),
1810:  \qquad r=1,2.
1811:  \end{aligned}
1812:  \label{eq:application-endpoint-states}
1813: \end{equation}
1814: Nothing here is a third convention: the package default is $s_\nu=+1$, with
1815: $h^{(r)}(\nu,0;z)=z^{+\nu}H^{(r)}_\nu(z)$ and
1816: $h^{(r)}(\nu,1;z)=z^{+\nu}H^{(r)}_{\nu-1}(z)$, and the two readings are related by the single
1817: simultaneous replacement $\nu\to-\nu$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:notation}, which must also be applied to the
1818: effective time powers below.
1819: Here $r$ selects the Hankel function and the binary index selects the
1820: undifferentiated or differentiated state. We use the same Hankel order
1821: at both ends of each line; constant factors from the physical mode
1822: normalisation are restored when forming the correlator.
1823: To display the time ordering explicitly, define
1824: \begin{equation}
1825:  \begin{aligned}
1826:  W^>_{\nu;ab}(s;\tau,\tau')
1827:  &=h^{(1)}(\nu,a;-s\tau)h^{(2)}(\nu,b;-s\tau'),\\
1828:  W^<_{\nu;ab}(s;\tau,\tau')
1829:  &=h^{(2)}(\nu,a;-s\tau)h^{(1)}(\nu,b;-s\tau'),
1830:  \qquad a,b\in\{0,1\}.
1831:  \end{aligned}
1832:  \label{eq:application-line-products}
1833: \end{equation}
1834: The corresponding line kernels, with mode prefactors removed, are
1835: \begin{equation}
1836:  \begin{aligned}
1837:  \mathcal G^{++}_{\nu;ab}
1838:  &=\theta(\tau-\tau')W^>_{\nu;ab}
1839:    +\theta(\tau'-\tau)W^<_{\nu;ab},\\
1840:  \mathcal G^{--}_{\nu;ab}
1841:  &=\theta(\tau-\tau')W^<_{\nu;ab}
1842:    +\theta(\tau'-\tau)W^>_{\nu;ab},\\
1843:  \mathcal G^{+-}_{\nu;ab}&=W^<_{\nu;ab},\qquad
1844:  \mathcal G^{-+}_{\nu;ab}=W^>_{\nu;ab}.
1845:  \end{aligned}
1846:  \label{eq:application-line-kernels}
1847: \end{equation}
1848: All kernels in this equation have arguments $(s;\tau,\tau')$.
